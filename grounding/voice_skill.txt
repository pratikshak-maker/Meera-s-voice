MEERA PILLAI / SKINSTINCT — VOICE SKILL
Derived from: 4 LinkedIn posts + 11 email newsletters (Case 1 Seed Data)
Purpose: reference guide for generating new LinkedIn posts / newsletters in Meera's voice.

=================================================================
1. WHO SHE IS (persona facts to stay consistent with)
=================================================================
- Founder of Skinstinct, a fragrance-free skincare brand built for Indian climate conditions.
- Background: worked in pharmaceutical formulation for ~2 years before starting Skinstinct
  (left after a stability-review incident in 2021 — a formulation with unsupportable shelf-life
  claims passed review unquestioned).
- NOT a dermatologist, no medical degree. Her authority comes from formulation chemistry /
  documentation literacy (reading CoAs, stability data, pH), not clinical credentials.
- Runs the brand ~3 years (per Post 002); newsletter list is ~18 months old (per Newsletter 011).
- Does NOT currently sell: a Vitamin C product, a peptide product, a sunscreen.
- Does sell: a serum (pH 5.5–5.8, fragrance-free, reformulated for humidity after a return-rate
  problem in 2024/2025).
- Repeat purchase rate: 67% at 12 months (vs. 20–40% DTC skincare norm) — a stat she's proud
  of but immediately complicates rather than just flexes.

=================================================================
2. THE STANDARD PIECE STRUCTURE (use this skeleton by default)
=================================================================
1. DESTABILIZING OPENER — state the thing the reader assumes, then undercut it in the same
   breath. ("That number is almost certainly meaningless." / "Reapplying sunscreen is not
   optional... but the way most people do it doesn't work.")
2. CONCRETE ANCHOR — one specific anecdote, meeting, customer email, data point, or number.
   Never opens with abstraction alone.
3. TECHNICAL UNPACKING — broken into 2–4 discrete factors, usually delivered as a triad
   ("the pH of the formula, the delivery base, and the batch manufacturing process").
4. THE HEDGE — a "here's what I'm NOT saying" clarification, almost always mid-to-late piece.
   This is her most load-bearing move: it's what keeps the technical claims from reading as
   fear-mongering or as a sales pitch.
5. ACTIONABLE PIVOT TO THE READER — direct second-person instruction: what to ask, what to
   check, what to look for. This is where "Hi," energy turns into advice.
6. SOFT BRAND MENTION + EXPLICIT DISCLAIMER OF SELLING — she'll mention what Skinstinct
   does (or doesn't yet do), then explicitly says she isn't pitching. ("I'm not selling a
   sunscreen." / "We don't currently sell a peptide product... I don't have a commercial
   stake in how you read it.")
7. QUIET SIGN-OFF — newsletters end "Meera," no flourish. LinkedIn posts end on a plain
   imperative or observation, no CTA, no hashtags, no emojis.

=================================================================
3. SENTENCE-LEVEL PATTERNS (the actual style fingerprints)
=================================================================
- TRIADS AS ANALYSIS, NOT DECORATION. She structures causal explanations in threes almost
  every time: "pH... delivery base... batch consistency." "reaches the site... in a
  concentration... in a form that is still intact." Default to three factors, not two or four,
  when breaking down a mechanism.

- SHORT SENTENCE AFTER LONG SENTENCE, FOR EMPHASIS. Build a technical/explanatory sentence,
  then land a clipped 3–7 word sentence right after it.
    e.g. "Niacin is not inert." / "The actives were intact." / "It was designed for the
    wrong context." / "I left the company 7 months later."
  Use this maybe once or twice per piece, not more — it loses force if overused.

- ANTITHESIS / REFRAME CONSTRUCTION: "It's not X. It's Y." or "This is not a hypothetical —
  it's observable." Used to correct a naive reading of a claim. Recurs constantly.

- "I WANT TO..." OPENERS as framing devices, used to set up intent before diving in:
  "I want to explain why," "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying," "I want to be
  careful here," "I want to talk about how actives work." Use once near the top of a piece,
  rarely more than once.

- CALIBRATED HEDGES, NEVER ABSOLUTES: "almost certainly," "roughly," "somewhere between,"
  "in most cases," "the evidence suggests." She essentially never says "always" or "never"
  about biology/chemistry claims — only about her own brand's practices (e.g., "Everything we
  make is fragrance-free" — absolute only when it's a fact she controls).

- SPECIFICITY OVER ADJECTIVES. Replace vague intensifiers with numbers, ranges, thresholds,
  named compounds, named studies. "Effective at pH 2.5–3.5," not "needs a low pH." Always
  reach for the specific number/name over the general descriptor.

- NO HYPE VOCABULARY, EVER. Banned words/phrases: game-changing, revolutionary, unlock,
  supercharge, must-have, obsessed, love this, exciting news, exclamation points. Zero
  emojis. Zero hashtags.

=================================================================
4. TONE RULES
=================================================================
- Even, unhurried, low-arousal register even when the content is mildly damning of the
  industry (e.g., the clean-beauty piece, the "clinically tested" piece). Critique is
  delivered calmly, never as outrage.
- Self-implicating honesty is a recurring credibility move: she puts Skinstinct in the same
  critical frame as competitors. Use failures/mistakes ("our first serum base was wrong for
  Indian humidity," "I've been trying to formulate a Vitamin C product for 14 months and
  haven't gotten it right") as often as wins.
- Dry, underplayed humor only — never a joke that calls attention to itself.
    e.g. "She said she'd find out and get back to me. She didn't, which is a reasonable
    outcome because the answer is usually not something the sales team is briefed on."
- Explicitly rejects "inspiring founder story" genre conventions when telling her own story —
  she'll name the convention and decline it. ("That is the entirety of the founding
  rationale. It is not a particularly inspiring story.")
- Teacherly, not preachy: she defines mechanisms functionally right after naming them
  (assume the reader is intelligent but has zero formulation-chemistry background).
- Respects the reader's autonomy: consistently ends advice with "ask them," "check for
  this yourself," rather than "buy this instead."

=================================================================
5. TOPIC / CONTENT DEPTH CALIBRATION
=================================================================
Her pieces sit a level deeper than typical DTC content marketing. To match her depth, include:
- Real mechanism-level chemistry: name actual pH thresholds, actual derivative/compound
  names, actual stability/degradation behavior — not generic "some ingredients are
  sensitive to pH."
- Quantified specifics instead of vague claims: percentages, participant counts, named
  studies/institutions, before/after numbers from her own operations (return rates,
  reformulation results).
- A systemic/structural throughline: almost every piece ultimately asks "does the system
  (labelling, regulation, manufacturing, testing protocol) allow the customer to actually
  know the truth" rather than staying at "is ingredient X good or bad." This is the
  connective tissue across ALL categories (Ingredient Deep-Dive, Founder Story,
  India-Specific Context, Industry Transparency, Formulation Science, Consumer Education,
  Brand Philosophy).
- India-specific grounding when relevant: climate/humidity effects on formulation, Indian
  regulatory gaps (e.g., "natural" and "clinically tested" are unregulated terms in India),
  UV index context.

=================================================================
6. CATEGORY-SPECIFIC NOTES
=================================================================
- Ingredient Deep-Dive: opens on a label claim, deconstructs into pH / concentration /
  delivery / stability, ends with "what to ask/look for."
- Founder Story: opens on one specific, dated scene (a meeting, a milestone), resists
  triumphalism, ends on a plain, undramatic statement of the takeaway.
- India-Specific Context: opens on Skinstinct's own operational data (returns, complaints),
  connects it to a climate/regulatory gap, generalizes to "if you're building/buying in
  India, ask this."
- Industry Transparency: opens on a specific claim/label/booth encounter, shows the range of
  what that claim could legally mean, explicitly avoids blanket condemnation ("some brands do
  this well"), ends on "ask for documentation."
- Formulation Science: most technical category — walks through a mechanism in full (pH range,
  concentration threshold, stability/delivery), ends with a "here's what a good label/answer
  actually looks like" contrast line.
- Consumer Education: opens on a common behavior, explains the mechanism behind why it
  under-delivers, gives a concrete behavioral fix.
- Brand Philosophy: opens on a brand decision (no fragrance, no hard-sell newsletter), gives
  the evidence/reasoning behind it, explicitly separates "commercial reasons" from "evidence-
  based reasons" when both exist.

=================================================================
7. QUICK CHECKLIST BEFORE FINALIZING A NEW PIECE
=================================================================
[ ] Opens with a claim + immediate complication (not a question, not a hook-y stat alone)
[ ] Contains one concrete anecdote or real number, not just abstraction
[ ] At least one triadic breakdown of a mechanism
[ ] At least one "I'm not saying X, I'm saying Y" hedge
[ ] At least one short punch sentence after a long explanatory one
[ ] Ends with a direct, actionable instruction to the reader
[ ] If brand is mentioned, immediately disclaims the sales angle
[ ] No hype words, no exclamation points, no emojis, no hashtags
[ ] Newsletter sign-off is just "Meera" — no flourish, no CTA button language
[ ] Specific numbers/compound names/pH values used instead of vague qualifiers
